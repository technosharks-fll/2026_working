WEATHER-AWARE GARDENING

Plant for the season, not just the forecast.

Use the local forecast as a guide, then follow seed-packet instructions and regional planting calendars. Allow soil to drain and avoid planting in extreme heat or before a hard frost.

SMALL CHANGES, REAL HABITAT

Ways to help from home.

Turn yards, balconies, gardens, and community spaces into safer places for wildlife. Choose an action that fits your space and local conditions.

✿
MAKE ROOM FOR POLLINATORS

Plant for the whole pollinator community.

Pollinators include bees, butterflies, moths, beetles, flies, wasps, hummingbirds, and bats. Provide a mix of native flowers, nesting areas, clean water, and bloom across the seasons.

❋

Plant native flowers

Choose locally native species and a variety of flower shapes and colors. Avoid plant material treated with neonicotinoids or other systemic pesticides.

⌁

Provide nesting places

Leave patches of bare soil, standing stems, leaf litter, and brush when safe and appropriate. Keep bird, bat, and insect homes out of direct disturbance.

≈

Add clean water

Use a shallow dish with stones or twigs for insects and small wildlife. Refresh and clean it regularly to prevent mosquitoes and disease.

◌

Leave the leaves

Many caterpillars, insects, and fungi overwinter in leaf litter and standing stems. Remove only what creates a documented safety, fire, or disease risk.

AVOID HARMFUL CHEMICALS

Pesticides can affect more than the target pest.

!

Before using a pesticide or herbicide: identify the problem, remove the cause where possible, and try non-chemical options first. Broad and systemic chemicals can poison pollinators, birds, pets, aquatic life, and soil organisms, and residues can move through water or food webs. Follow every label and local restriction, avoid treating flowering plants and active pollinator areas, and never apply more or closer than the label allows.

CHANGE THE LANDSCAPE

Replace lawn, one patch at a time.

♧

Create a native garden

Start with a small sunny or shady bed, group plants by water needs, and replace turf gradually. Local native-plant societies can help with species and sourcing.

☼

Try xeriscaping

Xeriscaping means thoughtful low-water landscaping—not covering everything in gravel. Improve soil, mulch, use efficient irrigation, and choose climate-appropriate native or adapted plants.

↯

Save water and soil

Catch and slow rainwater where allowed, keep mulch around plants, and reduce water runoff. Irrigate deeply and only as needed, especially before dry or hot weather.

CHOOSE ONE THIS WEEK
  • Plant one locally native, pollinator-supporting species.
  • Stop mowing or spraying one safe patch to let habitat grow.
  • Replace a small section of lawn with mulch and native plants.
  • Add a regularly cleaned shallow water source.
  • Ask a local garden center to label native and pesticide-free plants.

WILDLIFE SAFETY

Observe from a respectful distance

Give animals space

Use binoculars or a zoom lens. If an animal changes its behavior, you are too close—back away calmly.

Protect sensitive places

Do not publish nests, dens, rare-plant locations, or exact coordinates. Respect closures and private property.

Ask before identifying

Photos and community guesses are not confirmed identifications. Consult local experts and trusted field guides.
